Research question & system design01 / 05
Technical concept · Prepared for Emre Barut

From a point
to a paragraph.

Generate coherent text whose embedding lands near a chosen point on a two-dimensional semantic map—using a frozen embedding model and a frozen diffusion language model.

Start with approximately 1,000–2,000 texts, embed them with a fully accessible local encoder, and fit a fixed two-dimensional projection. A user clicks a location, then the system generates text, re-embeds it, and shows its actual map position. PCA is the cleanest first projection because its out-of-sample mapping and derivatives are explicit.

The central formulation

This is constrained generation. A map location defines a family of possible full embeddings and many possible texts. The objective is a plausible, useful member of that family within a declared tolerance. Exact matches at arbitrary clicks are not guaranteed.

SubproblemDecision to resolve
1. Target geometryDefine map distance; decide how to constrain or sample the unobserved dimensions.
2. Controlled generationConvert embedding error into revisions or token-level guidance without changing model weights.
3. Local executionChoose a Mac-compatible sampler, precision, model size, caching strategy, and compute budget.
4. EvaluationMeasure final-text accuracy, coherence, diversity, latency, and behavior at unreachable targets.
Working assumptions

Both models are downloadable and fully accessible. Gradients through the encoder’s inputs are available. Their weights stay fixed. The generation algorithm should accommodate different embedding models through an explicit interface.

What remains open

The residual-space prior, output length, text genre, acceptable error, and latency budget are not settled. M1–M5 support is a deployment goal; identical interactive performance across those machines is unverified.

Recommended starting point: implement the optimization interface first, compare gradient guidance with a scoring-only baseline, then choose the high-dimensional prior using measured results.

Subproblem 1 / What does a click specify?02 / 05

Two coordinates.
Many possible meanings.

Let e = E(x) ∈ ℝd be a text embedding, μ the corpus mean, and V the two orthonormal PCA directions. Preserve the exact preprocessing used for the corpus, including any normalization, prompts, pooling, scaling, and truncation.

π(e) = Vᵀ(e − μ),   y* ∈ ℝ²e = μ + V y* + (I − VVᵀ)rFor ordinary PCA without whitening, every r yields the same projected coordinates. This is an algebraic family, not a guarantee that any member is the embedding of natural text.

The missing dimensions can encode meaningful distinctions discarded by projection. Zeroing them gives a linear reconstruction, but may place the target away from the distribution of natural embeddings. Unit-normalized embeddings impose additional feasibility constraints. Replacing the encoder requires rebuilding its corpus embeddings and map; model independence means a reusable procedure, not invariant coordinates.

Option A · Optimize text directly

Minimize map error and add a plausibility or task constraint on the resulting full embedding. The generator contributes a language prior. No explicit full target vector is required, although the residual dimensions still need evaluation.

Option B · Complete a full target

Estimate a conditional residual distribution from nearby corpus examples, then choose or sample a residual before inversion. Local interpolation or a shrinkage Gaussian is a hypothesis to test; 1–2k texts do not justify a reliable unrestricted covariance in a large embedding space.

J(x) = ‖S[π(E(x)) − y*]‖² + α Rhigh(E(x); y*) + β Rquality(x)Proposed objective. S specifies axis weighting; α and β set trade-offs. Declare whether “close” means displayed map distance or variance-normalized distance.

Keep the map fixed during optimization

Refitting the projection moves the coordinate system and changes the task. Begin with PCA. Nonlinear projections require a defined out-of-sample transform; an inverse picture alone does not establish that a clicked location is semantically attainable.

Subproblem 2 / How the models connect03 / 05

Differentiate token choices.
Keep the weights frozen.

The optimization variables are per-position token logits. At a selected denoising step, the diffusion model predicts clean-token logits z⁰. Detach and copy them into z, which can be adjusted independently. For the simple bridge below, generator tokens correspond to encoder vocabulary entries; their internal vector spaces need not match.

qi = softmax(zi / τ),   ui = Σv qiv WE[v]e(z) = Ebody(u₁, …, uₙ)L(z) = ‖S[π(e(z)) − y*]‖² + αRhigh(e(z); y*)       + λ Σi KL(qi ‖ qi⁰)z ← z − η ∇zLWE is the encoder’s token lookup table. Ebody includes its fixed prefixes, pooling, and output normalization. q⁰ is the original generator distribution at the same temperature. This target-specific loss is our proposed adaptation.

The derivative travels from map error through the encoder, soft token mixtures, and softmax into z. The diffusion network supplies z⁰ but receives no backward pass. This is a local steering approximation, not an exact gradient through the final discrete generation. The KL term limits departure from the generator’s proposal; it does not guarantee fluent text.

Subproblem 3 / Compatibility & compute04 / 05

Spend gradients carefully.

Model independence has two levels

Scoring interface: any encoder that accepts text and returns vectors can score completed candidates. This supports arbitrary tokenizers.

Gradient adapter: expose input vectors and input gradients, preserve the encoder’s pooling and normalization, and map the generator’s vocabulary into encoder inputs. Model families and equal vocabulary sizes do not prove token-ID compatibility.

What tokenizer mismatch permits

EntRGi’s May 2026 revision maps overlapping tokens and sets non-overlapping token embeddings to zero; it reports useful guidance despite a large mismatch.[1] This is a lossy surrogate, not exact differentiable re-tokenization. Test coverage, special tokens, whitespace, and multilingual text. Always decode and re-tokenize normally for final scoring.

Prototype budget—not a measured optimum

Start with one encoder-loss update at 4–8 selected denoising steps. Sweep early, middle, late, and entropy-based schedules. Freeze model parameters but retain encoder input derivatives. Keep the generator outside autograd; avoid retaining its denoising history.

C ≈ T CG + K M (CE,fwd + CE,bwd) + CverifyAccounting model, not a benchmark. T: denoising passes; K: guided steps; M: updates per guided step. Prompt prefill is an additional cached setup cost. Top-k token mixtures reduce bridge costs, but may discard needed alternatives.

Cache corpus embeddings, the projection, and immutable prompt context. Recompute the changing text’s contextual encoder states as needed; those states generally cannot be reused unchanged. Compare a few candidate trajectories under equal wall-clock budgets. Guidance caching and sparse recomputation are relevant research directions, but sampler-specific integration is necessary.[2]

DiffusionGemma and the Mac constraint

Google’s released DiffusionGemma is a discrete, block-diffusion encoder–decoder with about 25.2B total parameters, 3.8B active parameters, and a 256-token canvas. Prompt context is cached, while the decoder repeatedly denoises the canvas.[7] Insert guidance before the sampler commits token decisions; do not assume a generic text-generation API exposes that hook.

A four-bit lower bound for 25.2B weights is 12.6 GB decimal (about 11.7 GiB), excluding quantization metadata, higher-precision layers, caches, activations, the encoder, and the operating system. Active parameter count reduces computation, not the need to store all experts. An 8 GB Mac cannot hold that weight lower bound; interactive performance on 16 GB machines is not established.

MLX is a candidate Apple-silicon backend; Google lists MLX among DiffusionGemma integrations.[8] MLX LM supports local LLM execution and quantization.[9] Verify the exact checkpoint, sampler hooks, encoder-input autograd, and operation support. Prefer one device stack to avoid repeated tensor transfers. Benchmark RAM and chip variants separately; GPU headline throughput is not a Mac estimate.

Subproblem 4 / Evidence & validation05 / 05

What is supported.
What to test next.

The literature supports inference-time steering and embedding inversion as components. This review did not establish an end-to-end solution combining arbitrary map clicks, interchangeable frozen encoders, DiffusionGemma, and interactive Mac execution. The experiment plan on page 2 makes that gap measurable.

Decision gate: continue with gradient guidance only if it improves decoded-text accuracy at an acceptable quality and latency budget. Establish output length, map tolerance, and response-time targets before judging success.
